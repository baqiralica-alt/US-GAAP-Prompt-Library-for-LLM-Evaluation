# Part C: Consolidation Prompts (ASC 810 with 805, 830, 740, 350, 230, 260)

IDs start with C. All figures are illustrative.

## Consolidation Workflow (use as a grading checklist)

1. **Which consolidation model?** Variable interest entity (VIE) analysis first (810-10-15-14), then the voting interest model (more than 50% and no participating rights held by others).
2. **Identify the acquirer and the acquisition date** (805-10-55).
3. **Measure at fair value:** consideration, previously held interest, non-controlling interest (NCI), identifiable assets and liabilities, including deferred taxes (805-740).
4. **Compute goodwill:** consideration + NCI fair value + fair value of previously held interest - fair value of net identifiable assets. US GAAP has no option to measure NCI at proportionate share (that is IFRS 3).
5. **Eliminate the investment account** against the subsidiary's equity and record fair value adjustments, goodwill and NCI.
6. **Eliminate intercompany balances and transactions:** sales, cost of sales, interest, fees, dividends, loans, receivables and payables, unrealized profit in inventory and fixed assets.
7. **Allocate net income** to controlling interest and NCI on the subsidiary's adjusted income (after fair-value adjustments and upstream profit eliminations).
8. **Translate foreign subsidiaries** if the functional currency differs (830).
9. **Prepare consolidated statements and notes:** NCI presentation within equity, cash flow classification, EPS, tax and goodwill disclosures.

# Part D: Master Case (Parent, Subsidiary, Consolidation)

Use this as a multi-turn prompt chain. Give the facts once, then ask Q1 to Q8 one at a time and grade each answer against the key.

## Facts

On 1 January 20X1, Parent P acquires 80% of Subsidiary S for **$1,760,000** cash. Tax rate is 25%.

- S's book equity: common stock $500,000; retained earnings $1,000,000 (total $1,500,000). S's cash at acquisition is $100,000.
- Fair values above book: equipment +$200,000 (10-year remaining life); unrecorded customer relationships $300,000 (5-year life). Everything else equals book value.
- NCI fair value: $440,000. Deferred taxes apply to the fair value step-ups (tax basis equals book value).
- 20X1 results: S's reported net income $400,000 (after a $50,000 management fee charged by P); P's standalone income, excluding any income from S, is $600,000 (including the fee). S pays $100,000 of dividends.
- S sold goods to P during 20X1 (cost $120,000, price $180,000). P still holds 25% of them at year-end.

## Questions and Answer Key

| Q | Question | Answer key |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Compute goodwill at acquisition. | Net identifiable assets = 1,500,000 + 200,000 + 300,000 - DTL (500,000 x 25% = 125,000) = 1,875,000. Goodwill = 1,760,000 + 440,000 - 1,875,000 = **$325,000** |

## Variations to Raise Difficulty (L5)

1. Change the NCI measurement to proportionate share and check the model flags that US GAAP does not allow it.
2. Make the inventory sale downstream and check that the NCI allocation changes.
3. Add a EUR functional currency to S and ask for the translation and CTA.

6. Add an impairment of S's goodwill and ask for the allocation between controlling interest and NCI.

# Part E: Scoring Rubric and Prompt-Writing Guidance

## Scoring Rubric (0 to 5 per dimension)

| Dimension | Weight | Full marks look like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct framework | 20% | Uses US GAAP and current ASUs; no IFRS leakage |
| Technical accuracy | 25% | Right recognition, measurement and classification; right deciding facts |
| Calculation and entries | 20% | Recomputes; debits equal credits; ties to financial statements |
| Reasoning | 15% | Steps shown; assumptions and judgments stated; right answer for the right reasons |
| Citations | 10% | Correct ASC paragraphs; none invented |
| Clarity | 10% | Plain English, ordered, auditable working |

Auto-fail triggers: unbalanced entry, IFRS-only answer given as US GAAP, invented ASC paragraph, wrong consolidation model.

## Writing Strong Evaluation Prompts

- Put the decision-driving fact in one sentence that is easy to miss (for example, a repurchase option or a participating right).
- Pair prompts: change one fact and see if the answer should change.
- Include an IFRS-looking trap on purpose (contingency midpoint, impairment reversal, NCI at proportionate share, development cost capitalization).
- Use round, checkable numbers; ask for entries and the ASC reference.
- Ask for assumptions when the facts are incomplete, and penalize invented facts.
- Record which trap the model fell into so patterns can be tracked.
